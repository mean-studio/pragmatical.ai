01 — The thesis

Nothing is more permanent than a temporary fix.

In software, the temporary fixes are the system. The bug patch from 2014. The cron job nobody owns. The spreadsheet on a network drive that quietly runs hundreds of millions in trades because someone hard-coded the path in 2007 and the people who knew it left.

Most large organisations now run on layers of temporary decisions that survived long enough to become infrastructure. The patches outlived the engineers. The documentation disappeared. The code stayed. And the conversation around AI in enterprise software has completely misunderstood where the real problem is.

The industry thinks the bottleneck is writing code. We do not. We think the bottleneck is understanding what you already have. That is the gap our four product lines are built to close — each one applying the same conviction to a different shape of the same problem.

The best developer reduces code and complexity.
They do not add more of it.

Contract Intelligence.

Enterprise contracts are written in natural language but tracked through human memory. Change tracking lives in email threads. Negotiation patterns live in legal counsel's head. The clause that was conceded in 2019 to close a deal becomes the standard nobody questions in 2026.

03. In development

Fleet Intelligence.

Route optimisation that works on the map fails in the city. Real urban terrain is one-way streets that change at school-run hour, weight restrictions written into council bye-laws, no-loading zones that the council updated last Tuesday, and the institutional knowledge of drivers who have been doing the route for fifteen years.

We extract the real-world constraints into a structured model — including the ones drivers carry in their heads — and produce routing that respects how the city actually works, not how the map says it should.

03 — How we work

One methodology, applied four ways.

Every product line follows the same arc, because the underlying problem is the same shape every time: messy real-world systems whose intent was never fully written down.

i

Map what exists.

Before transforming anything we extract what is already there — the code, the contracts, the routes, the data — into a canonical structured form. The map itself is usually the first thing the customer has ever had.

ii

Surface the patterns.

LLMs read across volumes humans cannot reasonably hold in their head at once. We use them for what they are genuinely good at — surfacing patterns, duplications, drift, dead code — never for confirming intent unsupervised.

iii

Confirm with humans.

For every pattern surfaced, a structured prompt goes to the person who still holds the knowledge. The engineer. The SME. The compliance officer. AI surfaces patterns. Humans confirm intent. We assume nothing.

AI surfaces patterns. Humans confirm intent.
The asset stays with you.

The output is not more code, more contracts, more dashboards. The output is a structured, machine-readable representation of how your business actually works — yours to own, yours to extend, yours to query with whatever AI you choose to use.
