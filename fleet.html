01 — The problem we are built for

Google Maps doesn't know your fleet. Your drivers do.

Generic routing engines compute the shortest path against a map. They do not know that the bridge has a 7.5-tonne weight limit enforced at the camera. They do not know that the school-run window turns the high street into a one-way trap between 8:20 and 9:15. They do not know that the warehouse loading bay only accepts deliveries on the eastbound approach because of how the kerb is cut.

Your drivers know all of this. The dispatcher who has been doing the patch for a decade knows all of this. None of it is in any system. When the dispatcher retires, the knowledge goes with them — and the new routing engine, computed against a clean map, makes the same mistakes the previous one did until the drivers manually re-learn the patch.

This is the same problem CodeDoctor solves for code. The intent — in this case, the operational reality of running vehicles in a real city — was never in the system. It was in the people. We extract it, validate it, encode it.

02 — How we approach it

Real-world constraints encoded into a structured model.

Extract the constraints. Validate them with the drivers and dispatchers who hold them. Encode the result as a portable model the optimisation engine respects.

  • i.
    Surface what is already known.

    Ingest telematics history, route logs, manual override records, dispatcher notes. Surface the patterns — the routes that drivers consistently deviate from the optimiser's recommendation, the times of day when the standard route breaks, the streets nobody uses despite the engine recommending them.

  • ii.
    Layer the public constraints.

    Council bye-laws, weight restrictions, loading-zone windows, ULEZ and clean-air-zone boundaries, road-works feeds, school zones. The constraints that exist on paper but no routing engine reads — until ours does.

  • iii.
    Validate with drivers.

    For every surfaced pattern, a structured prompt to the driver or dispatcher who runs that route. Why do you always take Brick Lane northbound here? Why does this depot always dispatch in this order on Tuesdays? The institutional answer becomes a structured constraint.

  • iv.
    Constraint rulebook.

    The output is a machine-readable rulebook of routing constraints — time-of-day rules, vehicle-class rules, kerb-side rules, driver-knowledge rules — that any optimisation engine can ingest. The asset stays with the fleet, not with the routing vendor.

03 — Where it fits

For fleets where the route isn't the optimisation problem — the terrain is.

  • 01.
    Urban last-mile and council fleets.

    Where the bye-laws, restrictions, and access rules are written into the municipality, not the map provider. Council waste collection. Last-mile delivery in central London, Manchester, Birmingham, Edinburgh.

  • 02.
    Specialist transport.

    Heavy goods, hazardous materials, abnormal loads — where vehicle-class constraints intersect with municipal restrictions in ways no consumer routing engine models correctly.

  • 03.
    Service-fleet operations.

    Engineers, technicians, healthcare home visits — where the optimum route depends on appointment windows, kerb access, parking realities, and dispatcher local knowledge that no generic routing model captures.
