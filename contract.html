01 — The problem we are built for

The archive is the source of truth. Nobody reads the archive.

Most enterprise legal functions operate from a position of partial recall. A given clause may have been conceded a hundred times, or twice, or never. Nobody knows without re-reading the archive. Nobody re-reads the archive.

The drift is invisible until something breaks. A supplier invokes a payment term that was negotiated three contracts ago and quietly carried into the template. A regulator asks how a clause is enforced across the portfolio and the answer is a six-week project. A new lawyer joins and rebuilds context from scratch, missing the constraints the previous lawyer encoded into the wording.

Contract review at scale is not a document-by-document problem. It is an archive-wide pattern problem. And the patterns are precisely what the team does not have time to extract.

02 — How we approach it

The same methodology that drives CodeDoctor, applied to legal text.

Extract structure. Surface patterns. Validate with humans who remember the negotiation. Encode the result as a rulebook the legal team owns.

  • i.
    Archive ingestion.

    Every contract in the archive — and every redlined draft, every email thread that surrounds it — extracted into a structured corpus. The first deliverable is a coherent picture of what has actually been agreed, by whom, when.

  • ii.
    Pattern surfacing.

    Clauses normalised. Variants identified. Concessions traced back through redline history. LLMs read across the archive at a scale no legal team can. We do not let them confirm anything unsupervised.

  • iii.
    Counsel-validated intent.

    For every surfaced pattern, the lawyer who negotiated it confirms or corrects what was actually intended. The reason the indemnity cap was halved in 2021. The lawful basis for the data-processing carve-out. The institutional memory becomes a structured asset.

  • iv.
    Clause rulebook.

    The output is a structured, machine-readable rulebook of clause equivalences, escalation paths, negotiation precedent, and standardisation decisions. Every entry traceable. Every entry exportable. Every entry queryable by whatever AI tools the legal function chooses to use.

03 — What it produces

The four deliverables of a Contract Intelligence engagement.

  • 01.
    Negotiation precedent map.

    For every standard clause, the record of where it has been negotiated, what was conceded, and which counterparties hold non-standard variants.

  • 02.
    Drift register.

    The clauses that have quietly migrated away from the template across years of negotiation. The places where the standard is no longer the standard.

  • 03.
    Counsel-validated rulebook.

    Every confirmed pattern, every standardisation decision, every escalation path encoded in a machine-readable format the legal function owns and extends.

  • 04.
    Queryable archive.

    The contract corpus exposed as a knowledge base that internal AI tools — copilots, compliance bots, deal-room assistants — can query against the firm's actual negotiated history, not a generic legal LLM.
